## Rethink Impact Prompt

Work through this before reading the rest of the chapter.

A recruiter fills in a signup form and submits it. The server responds with a 409 and the message: **"An account with that email already exists. Try logging in instead."**

Think through two separate questions.

### Question 1 — Is this actually a security problem?

The common answer is yes: returning that message enables account enumeration. An attacker can probe the API with a list of email addresses and learn which ones are registered users, enabling targeted phishing, credential stuffing, and social engineering.

That threat is real. But pause before accepting it as the only consideration.

**Context matters.** A job portal is a publicly visible platform. Recruiters post jobs with their company email addresses. Applicants publish profiles with their names and contact details. The privacy model here is closer to LinkedIn than to a banking portal. Users on this platform often *want* to be findable. The information that "alice@brightbuild.dev has an account" is not secret in the way that "alice@bank.com has an account" is secret.

**For whom is this a threat?** Account enumeration matters most when the fact of having an account is itself sensitive — a healthcare portal, an addiction recovery service, a dating platform. On a job portal, the link between a person and their account is one of the product's core value propositions.

**What does "no enumeration" actually cost?** A generic "something went wrong, please check your details" message on duplicate registration is confusing and increases support requests. Users who accidentally create a second account with a different email address are harder to help. The UX cost is real.

### Question 2 — What should your system actually do?

Three options:

**Option A — Short conflict message.** `409 { "code": "CONFLICT", "message": "Email already taken" }`. The machine-readable 409 status tells the client something is wrong. The message is brief but still confirms the email is occupied — "already taken" and "already exists" are functionally equivalent to an attacker probing the API. The tradeoff: a recruiter who genuinely forgot they signed up gets no guidance; the upside over Option B is slightly less explicit language.

**Option B — Helpful message on conflict.** `409 { "code": "CONFLICT", "message": "An account with that email already exists." }`. More honest. Confirms enumeration is possible through the API. Acceptable on a public-directory-style platform; not acceptable on a high-privacy platform.

**Option C — Silent success on conflict.** Return 200 and pretend the registration succeeded — perhaps send a "you already have an account" email to the address. This is the pattern used by high-privacy services: it leaks nothing through the HTTP response. The tradeoff: complexity, and it only works if you have a functioning email delivery pipeline.

**This chapter uses Option A** — `"Email already taken"` — as a direct conflict signal without confirming or denying that the email maps to a living account. The response reveals that the email is *occupied* in the system, which is a mild information leak, but one that is acceptable for this platform's threat model. If you were building a healthcare portal, you would use Option C.

The important thing is not which option you pick but that you pick deliberately, with the threat model in mind.
