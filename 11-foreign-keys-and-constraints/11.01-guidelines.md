# Guidelines — Chapter 11

## Before you start

You need the DDL sketches from:

- [Chapter 8](../08-model-accounts/) — `users`, `companies`, `recruiters`, `applicants`, `admins`
- [Chapter 9](../09-model-job-postings/) — `jobs`
- [Chapter 10](../10-model-applications/) — `applications`

Several FKs in those chapters were deliberately left without an `ON DELETE` clause. This chapter decides every one of them.

The next chapter, [Chapter 12](../12-migrations/), turns the complete DDL — FKs included — into versioned migration files. The decisions you record in your learning log here are what you'll implement there.

## Keep it yours

The FK decisions in this chapter are engineering trade-offs, not arbitrary defaults. Each one is a policy about what your portal does when data is deleted — so they belong to your schema design.

## Rethink impact

> Your schema has `ON DELETE CASCADE` on `applications.applicant_id`. An admin flags a user account as fraudulent and hard-deletes it. Every application that user ever submitted disappears silently from every recruiter's inbox. A job that showed 48 applications now shows 47. No error. No log entry. No way to recover.
>
> The admin didn't intend to destroy historical hiring data — they just wanted to block the account. What ON DELETE behaviour would have stopped this from happening automatically?
