# Choosing your stack & tools

The database is decided. Now you assemble the rest of the toolbox: the web framework, how you talk to PostgreSQL, how you validate input, and the supporting cast (Redis, a job queue, object storage). This is still a *decision* chapter — you won't install anything yet — and the goal is the same as Chapter 2: not just to pick tools, but to **be able to defend every pick** when someone asks "why this and not that?"

There's a quiet trap worth naming first. The tempting way to choose a stack is *"whatever the last tutorial used"* or *"whatever's trending this month."* That's how people end up running a tool they can't explain, can't debug, and can't justify in an interview. The professional way is the opposite: pick **boring, proven tools you can reason about**, and **write down why**. Newer is not better; *understood* is better.

## What you'll decide

By the end you'll have chosen — and recorded, with reasons — every major piece of your stack, on top of the **Node + TypeScript + PostgreSQL + Redis + S3-compatible storage + React/Next.js** base this course assumes. Four real decisions: the **language** (TS vs JS), the **web framework**, **database access**, and **validation** — plus the **supporting cast** you'll meet in later modules. For each, you'll learn the *axis* the choice turns on, so the decision is yours and you own it.

Then one habit that turns a thrown-together project into an engineered one: you'll record the whole thing as a set of short **Architecture Decision Records** in your repo.
