# Relationships and what's deferred

## Updated entity map

```
users (1) ──── (0..1) recruiters (N) ──── (1) companies (1) ──── (N) jobs   ← NEW
users (1) ──── (0..1) applicants
users (1) ──── (0..1) admins

jobs (1) ──── (N) applications      ← Chapter 10
applicants (1) ──── (N) applications ← Chapter 10
```

`jobs` now sits between `companies` (its owner) and `applications` (what it receives). The chain from recruiter to job to application flows through `company_id` at every step — a recruiter's jobs are scoped to their company, and applications are scoped to jobs.

## Rules the schema enforces now

| Rule | How it's enforced |
|---|---|
| Every job belongs to a company | `NOT NULL` on `jobs.company_id` |
| Status is one of three values | `CHECK` on `jobs.status` |
| `attributes` and `screening_questions` are valid JSON | Column type is `jsonb` — Postgres rejects non-JSON values |
| A job with no title can't be created | `NOT NULL` on `jobs.title` |

## What's deliberately NOT in this chapter

**`ON DELETE` for `company_id`** — what happens to jobs when a company is deleted? That decision belongs in [Chapter 11](../11-foreign-keys-and-constraints/).

**Indexes** — `jobs.status`, `jobs.deadline`, and `jobs.company_id` all need indexes. The public board queries on all three. [Chapter 42](../42-indexing/) covers this.

**Full-text search and JSONB filtering** — `title` and `description` will be searched; some `attributes` fields may become filterable. [Chapter 43](../43-search-and-filtering/).

**Cache for hot listings** — the public job board serves repeated reads of the same data. [Chapter 44](../44-caching/).

**Migrations** — [Chapter 12](../12-migrations/) turns this DDL into versioned migration files.
