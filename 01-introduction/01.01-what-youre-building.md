# The Job Portal Project

> You're building a real, deployed **job portal** — a place where companies post jobs and manage everyone who applies, while applicants browse the whole board, build a single shortlist of jobs spanning many companies, and apply once. It runs on a real URL, over HTTPS, with background workers humming behind it doing the slow work out of sight.

A job portal runs on one machine — companies post roles, applicants apply, recruiters review — but the *details* of a job change completely from one role to the next. An engineering role advertises a tech stack and a seniority level; a sales role advertises a commission structure and a territory; a design role links a portfolio. The job an illustrative role like *Senior Backend Engineer* carries — its salary, its skills, its own screening questions — is one example among many, not the shape of the whole course, and that role-by-role variety is exactly why a job's fields won't fit a single rigid set of columns.

## Make it yours

You name your own job portal, then carry that name through your commits, README, deploy URL, and case study — the course stops calling it "the job portal" the moment you've named it. Make the project genuinely yours in name and polish — the brand you choose and the care you put into the build are what turn a generic exercise into something you're proud to show.

## Introduction — what you'll build and why it matters

Almost any developer can build CRUD — create a row, read it, update it, delete it. Far fewer can answer the questions that actually decide whether software survives production: *What happens to this job board under load when a posting goes viral? How do you stop Company A from reading Company B's applicants? Where does the "your application was received" email get sent — and what happens when it fails?* Those questions **are** the job, and a job portal puts every one of them on the table at once.

This course is not about CRUD — you already have that. It's about everything that turns a CRUD app into a **production system**: configuration and secrets, real authentication and multi-tenant isolation, performance under load, moving slow work into background jobs, and shipping it so it stays up.

**When you're done**, you'll have a deployed job portal with three panels — **Admin, Company, and Applicant** — where companies manage their own roles and applicants, applicants browse across every company and apply through one shortlist, an admin moderates the platform across all companies, each application **snapshots** the résumé and profile at the moment it's submitted (so later edits never rewrite a sent application), and the slow work — confirmation emails, new-applicant alerts, résumé processing, scheduled recruiter digests — runs in background workers, behind caching, rate limiting, and logging you wouldn't be embarrassed to show in an interview.
