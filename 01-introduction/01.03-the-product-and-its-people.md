# The product, then the people

## The product — what you're building

A job portal is one public board with many company workspaces behind it. Each company opens a **workspace** where its recruiters post **jobs** — each with the role's details and its own screening questions, plus whatever attributes the role needs — and manage everyone who applies. Those attributes vary from one role to the next — an engineering role lists different things than a sales or design role — which is exactly why a job can't sit in one rigid set of columns. An **applicant** browses the whole board — every company's open roles in one place — searches and filters, and builds **one shortlist** that can hold roles from several companies at once. When they apply, that single shortlist becomes **one application per job** — each role has its own screening questions and is judged on its own merits, so the application is tied to the specific job, not lumped together by company (a recruiter simply sees their company's applications grouped in one place). At that moment the applicant's résumé and profile are **snapshotted** (frozen as a point-in-time copy), so later edits to the posting or the applicant's profile never rewrite an application already submitted. Behind all of it, an **admin** keeps the platform healthy — verifying companies, taking down bad postings, and watching platform-wide health.

Behind the scenes, the slow work — sending the applicant's confirmation email, alerting the recruiter to a new applicant, parsing and scanning the uploaded résumé, building the nightly recruiter digest — happens in **background workers**, off the request path, so the board stays fast.

Understand that shape first — one workspace per company, one shortlist across companies, one application per job at submission, the slow work pushed to workers — and the rest of the course is filling it in, properly, one production concern at a time.

## The people who use it

**Recruiter (at a company) — the Company panel.** Registers, opens their company's workspace, invites and manages team members (each with an assigned within-company role: owner, HR manager, recruiter, or hiring manager), posts and edits jobs (the role's details, its attributes, and its own screening questions), sees only their **own** company's jobs and applicants, reviews applications, moves applicants through interview stages (schedules interviews, records feedback, advances or rejects), and gets notified — and digested — about new applications.

**Applicant — the Applicant panel.** Registers, builds a profile (skills, portfolio links), browses the whole board, searches and filters, builds **one shortlist** across companies, uploads a résumé, answers each job's screening questions, applies once, and tracks where each application stands.

**Admin — the Admin panel.** Operates the platform: verifies and suspends companies, moderates and takes down job postings, suspends and reinstates users, and watches platform-wide health and stats. The admin is the one role that can see across every company — a deliberate, carefully-guarded exception to the isolation rule the rest of the system enforces.

Each role gets its own **panel** — Admin, Company, and Applicant — each built where its backend lives.
