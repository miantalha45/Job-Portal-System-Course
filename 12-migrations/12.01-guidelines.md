# Guidelines — Chapter 12

## Before you start

You need the complete DDL sketches from your learning log:

- [Chapter 8](../08-model-accounts/) — `users`, `companies`, `recruiters`, `applicants`, `admins`
- [Chapter 9](../09-model-job-postings/) — `jobs`
- [Chapter 10](../10-model-applications/) — `applications`
- [Chapter 11](../11-foreign-keys-and-constraints/) — all `ON DELETE` decisions recorded

Your `recruiters` DDL must include the `company_role` column from Chapter 8. If you skipped it or added it after the fact, verify it is there before writing any migration file.

The project directory structure from [Chapter 4](../04-project-bootstrap/) must be in place. The `migrations/` folder created in this chapter lives at the root of your project, alongside `src/`.

The next chapter, [Chapter 13](../13-seed-data/), assumes the migrations from this chapter have run cleanly and the schema exists.

## Rethink impact

> You've been working alone. Your schema works. Then a second developer joins. They clone the repo, start the database, and try to `psql` your SQL files in the order they found them in the `src/db/` folder. Two tables fail because `companies` has to exist before `recruiters`, and they guessed wrong. Six months later, you add a `deleted_at` column to `users`. There's no record of when it was added, whether it's been applied on staging, or whether their local database has it.
>
> What would have prevented both problems?
