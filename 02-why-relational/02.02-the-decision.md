# Why a relational database for a job portal

Before you write a single line of application code, you make a decision that quietly shapes every chapter after it: **what kind of database stores your job portal's data?** It's the foundation the whole build stands on — get it right and the hard chapters (the application transaction, multi-tenant isolation, the fast job board) get easier; get it wrong and you'll fight your own data layer for four weeks.

This is a *concept* chapter. There's nothing to build yet — the goal is that by the end you can **explain and defend** the choice, the way you'd have to in a design review. That matters, because "why did you pick this database?" is one of the most common questions a senior engineer will ask you, and "it's what I knew" is not an answer that lands.

## The decision in front of you

Every database splits, at the top level, into two broad families:

- **Relational (SQL) databases** — PostgreSQL, MySQL, MariaDB. Data lives in **tables** (rows and columns) with a defined **schema** — a fixed shape the database knows and enforces. Rows are linked to each other by **keys**, and the database itself enforces rules about those links. You query with SQL.
- **Document (NoSQL) databases** — MongoDB, DynamoDB, Firestore. Data lives in flexible **documents** (think JSON objects), usually with **no enforced schema** — each document can look however it likes. Related data is often **nested inside** a single document rather than linked across separate tables.

Two new words there, defined as we go: a **schema** is the agreed shape of your data — which fields exist, of what type, and which are required — that the relational database refuses to let you violate. **Referential integrity** (coming up) is the database guaranteeing that a link from one row to another actually points at something real.

Both families are good tools, used in production at huge scale. The question is never "which is better" in the abstract — it's **which fits a job portal**, where companies post jobs, applicants apply, and recruiters review. So let's reason it out properly, the way you'll have to defend it later.
