## What this chapter builds

Two ownership-check functions:

**`assertJobOwnership(jobId, companyId)`** in `src/modules/jobs/jobs.repo.ts` — fetches the job row and verifies its `company_id` matches the recruiter's `companyId`. Throws `NotFoundError` if the job does not exist or belongs to a different company.

**`assertApplicantOwnership(applicantId, userId)`** in `src/modules/applicants/applicants.repo.ts` — fetches the applicant row and verifies its `user_id` matches `req.user.userId`. Throws `NotFoundError` if the applicant record does not exist or belongs to a different user.

A demonstration route `GET /api/jobs/:id` wired to use `assertJobOwnership` shows the complete pattern: role gate → company resolution → ownership check → handler.

## The IDOR problem this chapter solves

After Chapter 28, `GET /api/companies/me` is safe because it never uses a URL parameter for scoping. But most routes need to act on a specific resource: `GET /api/jobs/:id`, `PUT /api/jobs/:id`, `DELETE /api/jobs/:id`. The `:id` in the URL is necessary — you need to know which job is being requested.

The problem: a recruiter from Company A who knows the UUID of a Company B job can call `GET /api/jobs/<company-B-job-id>`. If the handler only queries `WHERE id = $1` without also checking `company_id`, Company A's recruiter reads Company B's data. That is IDOR: the object is referenced by a direct ID in the URL, and the server did not check whether the caller owns it.

`assertJobOwnership` is the check that closes this gap.
